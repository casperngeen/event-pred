# Research Update — Proposed Refinements to the CA Report Design

**Ng E En Casper** · *AI in Financial Data Analytics and Trading:
Analysing Cross-Market Influence Propagation in Event Prediction Markets via
Spatial-Temporal Graphs*

---

## Purpose of this document

The CA report set out four research components and a proposed model architecture.
Since submission I have completed the data pipeline work that was listed as
outstanding, and used the resulting panel to run a series of measurement and
feasibility tests. Those tests have clarified several design decisions that the CA
report left open or under-specified.

This document sets out **seven proposed refinements**, each with the motivation and
the supporting measurement. The research question, the four components, and the
overall framing are unchanged. What changes is the estimator for the structural
claim, the statistical discipline around it, and how the trading evaluation is
framed.

**Scope caveat applying to everything below.** All analysis is **in-sample only**
(pre-2026). The 2026 block is held out and untouched, enforced by a single
in-sample/out-of-sample boundary applied uniformly across the analysis. Several
results below are in-sample-generated hypotheses and are labelled as such; the
out-of-sample confirmation is deliberately reserved for a single test at the end
of the project.

---

## 1. Contract-type handling: a PMF path for bucket ladders

**Status: implemented.**

### Motivation

Kalshi macro markets come in three structurally different shapes, and the CA
report's §3.1 acknowledges this ("heterogeneity in submarket structure presents a
non-trivial challenge"). The pipeline as submitted treated all ladders as
cumulative threshold ladders. That is correct for CPI, U3, GDP and payrolls, but
wrong for bucket contracts, which price P(a ≤ X ≤ b) directly — they are already
a probability mass function and need normalisation, not the successive
differencing a cumulative ladder requires.

Measured composition of the macro universe: **5,169 threshold / 7,875 bucket / 204
categorical**. WTI is **85% bucket** (7,818 of 9,222 contracts), and was therefore
absent from implied-mean output entirely rather than merely noisy.

### What was done

A bucket PMF path now normalises bucket legs over their midpoints and handles the
two open tails separately. The WTI ladder structure is clean and regular — verified
across all 702 events, typically 13 unit-width interior buckets ("$62 to 62.99",
"$63 to 63.99", and so on) plus two open tails ("$61.99 or below", "$75.0 or
above").

### Validation

| check | WTI | WTIW |
|---|---|---|
| events with a usable PMF | 264 / 702 | 38 |
| corr(implied mean, resolved value) | **0.990** | 0.953 |
| median \|surprise\| | $0.43/bbl | $0.86/bbl |
| events with \|surprise\| > $5 | 0 | 1 |

Three further defects in threshold parsing were corrected in the same pass, all of
which had been failing silently rather than raising an error. Strike thresholds
were being dropped for entire series (jobless claims 0 of 132 parsed, ISM PMI 0 of
49 — now 100% coverage across all threshold ladders). Recovery of the resolved
value assumed a fixed strike spacing of 0.1, which is correct for CPI, U3 and GDP
but wrong by roughly six orders of magnitude for payrolls, where strikes are
spaced ~50,000 apart. And inclusive and exclusive threshold conventions coexisted
without normalisation ("Above 4.3%" against "At least 225000" against "216,000 or
above"), so nominally identical strikes were being treated as different
quantities.

### An unexpected by-product worth reporting

Normalised ladder mass has a **median of 1.28** — WTI ladder prices sum to 128% of
certainty. This is a directly measurable coherence violation, and it is a candidate
standalone contribution: the degree to which these markets fail internal
arithmetic consistency is quantifiable and, as far as I can find, unreported.

## 2. Structure estimation as the primary deliverable, with AGCRN repositioned

**This is the most substantive of the proposed changes.**

### Motivation

§3.2 states the gap as: *"no existing work has characterised whether these
cross-market belief updates are **structured**."* Characterising that structure is
the contribution. AGCRN was proposed as the mechanism for recovering it, on the
grounds that inter-market relationships are latent.

Three measurements suggest the structure is better estimated directly, and that
AGCRN is better positioned as an object of study than as the primary model.

**(a) The structure is recoverable by direct estimation, and direct estimation
yields something a learned adjacency cannot.** §4.3.1 already recovered
interpretable relationships with elementary event-study statistics. An edge
estimated directly carries a point estimate, a standard error, and an
FDR-corrected p-value — so it can be reported as a finding and confirmed
out-of-sample. An adjacency optimised against forecasting loss has no confidence
interval and no independent test.

**(b) Capacity against available supervision.** Parameter count for the proposed
architecture, against the labels the panel actually supplies:

| configuration | parameters | params / label |
|---|---|---|
| c=10, d=2, hidden=16 (deliberately minimal) | 3,477 | 0.4 |
| **c=10, d=10, hidden=64 (Bai et al. default)** | **145,549** | **15.6** |
| c=10, d=10, hidden=128 | 535,501 | 57.4 |

The panel supplies 3,108 fully-labelled node-snapshots × 3 horizons = **9,324
scalar labels**. Labels are also not independent: intra-snapshot ICC is 0.309,
giving a design effect of 2.30 and an effective n of 1,353 — **36 parameters per
effective label** at the default configuration. Separately, node-adaptive
parameter learning exists to let *persistent* nodes specialise, and in the
inductive DTDG setting node identities do not persist across snapshots, which
weakens that mechanism's motivation independently of sample size.

**(c) An architectural mismatch with the empirical finding.** The adaptive
adjacency is Ã = softmax(ReLU(EEᵀ)). ReLU zeroes negative inner products and
softmax makes every entry positive and row-stochastic, so **Ã cannot represent a
negative edge**. The strongest directional finding in the data is that
unemployment surprises carry the *opposite* sign to inflation surprises (higher
unemployment is dovish). That sign cannot live in the adjacency; it must be
carried by the convolution weights instead. This is independent of any sample-size
argument.

**(d) A complexity ladder run at linear capacity finds nothing for the graph term
to add.** Walk-forward, 8 expanding folds within the in-sample block, 782 pooled
holdout rows, predicting the 3-day implied-mean change:

| rung | MAE | RMSE | R² vs zero | dir. acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| predict no change | 5.66 | 8.57 | 0.0000 | — |
| own price level | 5.75 | 8.59 | −0.0065 | 48.5% |
| own price level + 3-day momentum | 5.75 | 8.62 | −0.0118 | 48.3% |
| + neighbour momentum (all nodes) | 5.79 | 8.67 | −0.0234 | 48.0% |
| + neighbour momentum (same series) | 5.79 | 8.63 | −0.0157 | 47.1% |

Every rung is worse than predicting zero out of sample, and the graph terms carry
*negative* incremental R² (−0.004 to −0.068). This does not prove a graph network
would fail, but it means there is no linear-capacity signal in the neighbour
channel for a higher-capacity model to build on.

### Proposed change

1. **Stage 1 (primary): direct structure estimation.** Full ordered trigger→target
   grid, sign/rank statistics, FDR correction, permutation verification, then a
   single out-of-sample confirmation. Deliverable is a **validated adjacency
   matrix** — per-edge estimate, CI, corrected p-value.
2. **Stage 2: AGCRN as a validation study rather than the primary model.** Train
   it, then compare its learned adjacency against the directly estimated one. If
   the learned adjacency recovers the FDR-validated edges, that is evidence
   adaptive graph learning works in a low-data regime. If it places weight on pairs
   the grid rejects, that is a concrete finding about a method the STG literature
   applies without validation. Either outcome is reportable, and neither requires
   the model to be competitive for the project to succeed.

This keeps AGCRN in the thesis and keeps the methodological contribution, while
removing the dependency of the whole project on a single model training well on
~400 events.

## 3. Multiplicity control, and reporting how many pairs were searched

### Motivation

§4.3.1 identifies 22 significant relationships, defined as pairs where the 95% CI
on the mean price change excludes zero **for at least three consecutive days**. The
paths are cumulative sums normalised to zero at day 0, so consecutive days are
near-perfectly autocorrelated: once the CI excludes zero on one day it is very
likely to on the next. The consecutive-day rule therefore does not function as a
multiple-comparison guard.

Monte Carlo under a pure random walk, measuring the per-pair false-positive rate
of exactly this screening rule:

| pairs per test (n) | P(false positive) |
|---|---|
| 5 | 0.183 |
| 10 | 0.121 |
| 28 | 0.091 |
| 100 | 0.078 |
| 811 | 0.074 |

At a 7–18% per-pair rate, an exhaustive grid of 506 ordered pairs would be expected
to produce roughly **52 false positives** — more than the 22 reported. This does
not establish that the 22 are spurious; it establishes that the screen cannot
distinguish them from noise, and that the number of pairs searched must be
reported alongside the number found.

### Proposed change

Report the search breadth explicitly, and apply Benjamini–Hochberg across the full
grid. Results of doing so are in §4.

## 4. Stage-1 structure estimation: first results

Full ordered grid, in-sample, dormant horizon, Spearman rank statistics with the
direction read off the estimate rather than imposed.

- **149 ordered pairs searched** (n ≥ 10)
- 16 significant uncorrected at p<0.05, against **7.5 expected by chance**
- **5 edges survive Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.10**, all confirmed by permutation

| edge | n | ρ | permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPICORE → CPI | 24 | 0.744 | 0.0000 |
| PROLLS → FED | 10 | 0.879 | 0.0005 |
| **CPI → FED** | 46 | 0.492 | 0.0010 |
| CPIYOY → PCECORE | 12 | 0.720 | 0.0095 |
| **CPICORE → FED** | 39 | 0.465 | 0.0030 |

An earlier, independent sign-based test on theory-specified cells gives a
consistent picture: pooled sign agreement 66.4% (n=149 deduplicated) against a
block-permutation null, p = 0.0012. The signal is in the **direction** of the
surprise, not its magnitude — a cross-check between CPI and CPIYOY (separate
ladders resolving from the same BLS print) gives r = 0.686 on signed surprise but
only r = 0.242 on absolute surprise.

### Two observations that shape the next phase

**Roughly half the surviving edges are same-release pairs.** CPICORE→CPI and
CPIYOY→PCECORE connect contracts that resolve from the same print on the same
morning. That is not information propagating through the market; it is two
contracts reading one number. The genuinely cross-domain edges are **CPI→FED** and
**CPICORE→FED**. This makes the identification problem concrete rather than
abstract, and motivates §7 below.

**No evidence of multi-hop routing.** Testing whether A→C attenuates once B is
controlled for — the specific question that distinguishes a graph from a list of
bilateral edges:

| A → C (controlling B) | n | ρ(A,C) | partial | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WTI→FED \| CPI | 20 | −0.495 | −0.473 | +0.021 |
| CPI→FED \| CPIYOY | 25 | 0.349 | 0.447 | +0.098 |
| PAYROLLS→FED \| U3 | 14 | 0.609 | 0.650 | +0.041 |
| CPI→FED \| PAYROLLS | 14 | 0.451 | 0.506 | +0.055 |

Every partial correlation is unchanged or slightly stronger. With n = 14–25 this
has very little power, so this is *no evidence of* multi-hop structure rather than
evidence of its absence. It is nonetheless the right test, and it is the one that
determines whether message passing across hops has anything to operate on.

### On WTI specifically

Now that bucket contracts parse, WTI can enter the universe — it was the
highest-n trigger in the original sweep. Adding it increases the pair count by
**+217%** but the count of distinct target contracts by only **+39%** (WTI→FED
gives 261 pairs across just 20 target contracts, because WTI resolves weekly while
the FOMC meets eight times a year). The WTI cells themselves are a clean null:
sign agreement 49.1% (p = 0.67), ρ = −0.021 (p = 0.69), and this is robust to every
data-quality filter I applied (≥8 legs, coherent ladder mass, above-median
surprise).

An economically defensible reading exists alongside the statistical one: oil is a
supply shock, and central banks routinely look through it, so WTI→FED was always
the weakest transmission story on the list. I propose reporting the WTI null as a
result rather than dropping it.

## 5. Fixing the response-window convention explicitly

### Motivation

Three different measurement windows are currently in use across the analysis, and
they are not interchangeable:

| convention | definition | pooled sign agreement |
|---|---|---|
| **dormant** | trigger resolution → 3rd subsequent trade | 66.4% (p = 0.001) |
| **liquid** | trigger resolution → mean of target's final 7 days | 59.3% (p = 0.04–0.09) |
| **CA report §4.3.1** | [−3, +14] calendar days around the trigger | — |

Measured timing of the dormant window: median length **0.5 hours**, against a
median 20.2 days from trigger resolution to the target's own close. The window
therefore covers about **0.1%** of the target's remaining life. The entry
observation is also stale by a median of 6.8 hours.

The liquidity-gated hypothesis — that information sits unpriced until the target
market becomes active — predicts liquid should dominate. It does not (66.4% vs
59.3%), and the liquid result does not survive the corrected null. This argues for
fast partial repricing rather than delayed incorporation, and I propose reporting
it as a tested and rejected alternative rather than dropping it silently.

### Proposed change

Adopt the dormant window as the primary definition, justify it explicitly against
the alternatives, and report the liquid result as a robustness check. Also worth
noting: 48% of the signed move occurs in the *first* print after resolution
(median 12 minutes later), and the third trade contributes essentially nothing
(−0.08¢ of a +0.57¢ total), so the window could be shortened to two trades with no
loss of information.

## 6. Execution assumptions, and reframing the trading evaluation

### Motivation

§5.4 proposes integrating STG-derived signals into PortfolioBench and assessing
whether they "translate into economically meaningful trading outcomes", but does
not state any execution assumptions. Having now measured the frictions, those
assumptions turn out to be decisive, and I propose making them explicit and
reframing the evaluation accordingly.

### Measured frictions

Order-book data covers one macro contract (the January 2026 FOMC "no change"
contract, 117,769
snapshots over five weeks, 56–91 days to close), so these are indicative rather
than representative — widening this measurement is a live task.

| spread basis | value |
|---|---|
| all book snapshots | 8.30¢ mean / 8.0¢ median |
| time-weighted | 7.18¢ |
| **at the instants trades actually occurred** | **4.69¢ mean / 4.0¢ median** |
| effective half-spread paid vs prevailing mid | 2.74¢ (buy yes) / 3.21¢ (buy no) |

Matching each trade to the prevailing book snapshot is the measurement that
matters for execution, and it shows trade-based estimators are biased low by
roughly **1.8×** — not the 8× that comparison against a naive bounce estimator
suggested.

Spreads also tighten materially toward resolution. Within the one contract, mean
spread falls from 12.82¢ at 85–90 days to close to **2.64¢ at 55–60 days**. Across
a cross-section of live quotes, open interest tracks the spread more closely than
days-to-close does (OI ≥ 1k → 3¢ median; OI < 1k → 6¢). This matters because
activity is already known to concentrate in the final ~14 days, so the tradeable
window and the tight-spread window coincide.

### The feasibility result

Kalshi charges a taker fee of 7¢ × p(1−p) per contract, maximised at 1.75¢ for an
at-the-money strike. A perfect-foresight bound — take the realised direction every
time, which no model can beat — gives:

| universe | n | E\|response\| | 95% CI | vs 3.50¢ round-trip fees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| theory-specified cells | 355 | 2.39¢ | [1.98, **2.79**] | excluded |
| widened with WTI | 1,125 | 2.07¢ | [1.87, **2.27**] | excluded, more tightly |

At the dormant horizon the upper bound on what a *perfect* forecaster could earn
sits below the exchange fees alone, with the spread set to zero. This is a
model-free statement and it is well-powered: because it averages absolute moves
rather than signed ones, E|response| is estimated 5–9× more precisely than the
signed edge.

Holding to settlement rather than round-tripping is the better execution — it pays
entry only and captures 5.5× the move (13.12¢ vs 2.39¢). It does not change the
conclusion: pre-cost edge +1.08¢ (t = 0.91), −1.83¢ after entry cost, against
control rules requiring no model at all ("always buy the favourite", +1.19¢). The
binary payoff also raises per-trade standard deviation from 4.6¢ to 22.6¢,
requiring ~1,764 observations to detect the pre-cost edge at t = 2 against **368**
available in four years of in-sample data.

Two levers were tested. Moving off at-the-money strikes to reduce fees does not
work — the fee falls 12× toward the tails but the edge falls faster, and 91% of
tradeable strikes sit in a bucket with an edge of −0.19¢. Selectivity does work:
restricting to the top decile of surprise magnitude raises gross edge from 0.49¢
to 2.28¢ (se 1.33), which is the surprise-scaling prediction operating at strike
level.

### Proposed change

Reframe the trading evaluation from a profitability test to a test of **economic
significance**: how large is the cross-market drift relative to the frictions that
sustain it?

The motivation is that the hypothesis and the friction are linked. The drift is
argued to persist because books are thin, no arbitrageur enforces cross-market
consistency, and participants have limited attention — and those are the same
conditions that produce a wide spread. **The friction that creates the signal is
related to the friction that eats it.** A large drift behind a tight spread would
be the surprising result, and would more likely indicate measurement error than
free money.

Framed this way, both outcomes are results, and the current one quantifies the
limits to arbitrage that explain the drift's persistence. It also decouples the
two deliverables: the structural claim no longer depends on a model trading well.
Angelini & De Angelis (2026) reach a comparable conclusion on Kalshi, which
supports the framing and makes differentiating from them more urgent — the
differentiation is the cross-market channel and the quantified ceiling, not the
negative result itself.

## 7. Elevating identification to a priority

### Motivation

Nothing in the analysis so far separates *"information propagated through the
market"* from *"both contracts respond to the same public release"*. §4 makes this
concrete: roughly half the FDR-surviving edges connect contracts resolving from
the same print. A sign test cannot address this, and the strength of the whole
structural claim depends on it.

### Proposed approach

Two routes, both feasible with data in hand or cheaply obtainable:

1. **Release-vs-resolution timestamps.** Where the trigger's underlying data
   release and the contract's resolution occur at different times, genuine
   propagation should track the release, while a common-signal confound tracks
   neither differentially.
2. **CPI subcomponent mechanical-weight decomposition.** Headline CPI is a known
   weighted sum of its subcomponents. The mechanically-implied response is
   computable, so any *excess* response is the candidate propagation effect.

## What is unchanged

- The research question and the four research components.
- The DTDG / inductive framing. Node membership genuinely does vary across
  snapshots, and shared-MLP node representation remains the right response.
- The modular graph construction framework, with interchangeable node, edge,
  temporal and label strategies. This is what makes the ablation ladder tractable,
  and it is unaffected by any of the proposals above.
- PortfolioBench as the evaluation layer.
- AGCRN as a component of the thesis — repositioned, not removed.

---

## Still outstanding

- **Out-of-sample confirmation.** Everything above is in-sample and
  hypothesis-generating, including a documented specification search
  (magnitude → signed → sign-only). The 2026 block is untouched and reserved for a
  single confirmatory test at the end.
- **Identification design** (§7) — the highest-value open item.
- **Full grid re-estimation** now that bucket ladders are handled, since the
  original sweep predates that correction.
- **Widening the spread measurement** beyond the single contract currently
  available, which is the load-bearing assumption behind §6.
- **Validation checks on the data pipeline.** The parsing defects corrected this
  cycle all failed silently rather than raising an error, and each would have been
  caught by a basic coverage check. Putting those checks in place is the first
  task, since every result above depends on the measurement layer being sound.
