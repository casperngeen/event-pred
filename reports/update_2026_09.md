## Purpose of this document

The August update proposed seven refinements to the CA report design:
- a probability-mass path for bucket contracts;
- direct structure estimation as the primary deliverable, with AGCRN
  repositioned as a validation study;
- multiplicity control;
- first structural results;
- a fixed response window;
- reframing the trading evaluation as a test of economic significance;
- elevating identification to a priority.

This document reports what happened to each of them during September, and
what else the month produced. The period covers:
- an audit of the data path that corrected nine defects;
- a revised structural result;
- a direction-prediction study, and its withdrawal;
- a full economic evaluation;
- a trading specification frozen for one out-of-sample test;
- a sequence of studies that settles the spatial-temporal graph (STG)
  question.

**Scope caveat applying to everything below.** All analysis is **in-sample**
(October 2021 to December 2025). The 2026 block is untouched, enforced by one
in-sample/out-of-sample boundary that every analysis script checks. The inputs
for the single out-of-sample test have been built (§6.4). No out-of-sample
result has been computed.

### Terms used throughout

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| trigger / target | A **trigger** is a contract whose resolution is the news (e.g. the CPI print). A **target** is another market whose price may respond (e.g. the Fed-rate ladder) |
| surprise, z | The trigger's resolved value minus the market's implied mean just before resolution, standardised per series. This is the *market-relative* surprise, as opposed to a survey-consensus one (§3.3) |
| theory sign | Fixed before looking at any estimate. A hawkish surprise (higher inflation or payrolls, lower unemployment or jobless claims) should raise policy-rate expectations, and the reverse for dovish. It is used as a zero-parameter sign restriction |
| channel | A source type → target type group of pairs, e.g. labour → policy |
| BH | Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery control, at q = 0.10 |
| same-release pair | Two contracts that resolve from the same print (e.g. CPI and core CPI). A relation between them is arithmetic, not propagation |
| R² vs zero, AUC | Out-of-sample fit against predicting no change, and ranking accuracy of the direction (0.5 = chance) |

## 2. The measurement layer: an audit and nine corrections

A review of the data path and the study code found nine defects. All of them
failed silently rather than raising an error. Each now has a regression test.

- **The resolved value was inferred when the true value was on disk.** Each
  event's outcome had been reconstructed from the ladder, to a precision of
  half a strike spacing. For CPI that error is 0.05pp, which is **68% of the
  median surprise being measured**. Meanwhile, the exchange's printed
  settlement value had been downloaded for 1,022 events and was never read.
  It is now used for 442 of 519 panel rows, and surprises moved by a median
  50–67% of their previous magnitude.
- **The implied distributions were not checked for being distributions.**
  Coverage and ladder-mass diagnostics were computed and never enforced. A WTI
  ladder whose mutually exclusive buckets summed to 2.96 was accepted and
  divided by its own total. Two-sided gates now apply to both contract paths.
  This cost 799 → 519 rows and no trigger series.
- **Two panels applied opposite staleness rules.** As a result, 74.5% of
  node-feature rows were built from legs last traded on different days.
- **Five defects in the rank statistics:**
  - Spearman ranks ignored ties, on data that is 98% tied (max |Δρ| = 0.24);
  - the p-value used a normal where a t belongs;
  - BH controlled the wrong p-value;
  - the permutation p could be exactly zero;
  - one shared random generator made every p-value depend on call order.
- **A look-ahead in instrument choice.** The target leg used to measure each
  response was chosen using trades after the decision. It was holding up the
  strongest same-release edge.
- **Three smaller defects:**
  - a cache served an eight-day-old panel through two rounds of fixes;
  - rolling windows counted rows rather than days;
  - one aggregation standardised against the future.

**One data-quality finding, not defects in the code:**
- **Simultaneous trades.** 47% of trades on target contracts share a
  timestamp with another trade (one order sweeping several price levels). So
  "the last trade before X" was not well defined, and had been resolved by
  input order. Every such rule is now deterministic. The trading headline
  moved as a result (§6.2).

## 3. The structural result

### 3.1 Stage 1: four edges, all macro → policy

For each ordered (trigger, target) pair, the statistic is the signed rank
correlation between the trigger's surprise and the target's post-resolution
response. Selection uses BH over the full grid on an exact permutation p.

- **141 ordered pairs searched.**
- 13 are nominally significant at p < 0.05, against **7.1 expected by
  chance**.
- **4 survive BH at q = 0.10:**

| trigger | target | n | ρ̂ | permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPI | Fed rate | 36 | +0.617 | 0.0005 |
| Payrolls | Fed decision (hike) | 26 | +0.596 | 0.0005 |
| Payrolls | Fed rate | 30 | +0.583 | 0.0010 |
| Core CPI YoY | Fed decision (cut) | 17 | −0.683 | 0.0025 |

**Four is a stronger result than August's five, not a retreat.** The two
same-release edges (CPICORE → CPI, CPIYOY → PCECORE), which the August update
flagged as "two contracts reading one number", fell out once:
- the instrument look-ahead was removed;
- exact rather than asymptotic p-values were used;
- the distributions were gated.

What remains is the scheduled macro surprise → policy path channel, the
best-documented transmission channel in macro-finance (Kuttner 2001;
Gürkaynak, Sack & Swanson 2005). **Identification is no longer load-bearing on
the headline.**

### 3.2 Sign restrictions pass, including the falsification cell

Signs were fixed from theory before the estimates were consulted. Every
hawkish channel is positive. Both dovish unemployment-rate cells flip, as
required, and the rate-cut side of the Fed decision market carries the
opposite sign. This check would catch an estimator that fits generic
co-movement, and no validation loss can provide it.

### 3.3 Robustness: the edges are robust; the BH count is not

- **Three alternative surprise measures.** These are a quantile (PIT)
  surprise, surprisal, and a variant restricted to events the ladder could
  express. No edge disappears; ρ falls by 0.04–0.19. But the BH count goes
  4 → 1 → 1 → 0. Part of the fall is resolution: the permutation floor
  (0.0005) sits just under BH's rank-1 threshold (0.00071).
- **A survey-consensus surprise** (`actual − consensus`, the event-study
  standard). The consensus comes from an economic calendar matched to each
  Kalshi event; 222 of 225 matched values agree with Kalshi's settlement.
  - The two surprises agree: per-series Spearman +0.84 to +0.96, and sign
    agreement 0.90.
  - Across all 93 comparable pairs, the correlation between the market-based
    and consensus-based ρ̂ is **+0.90**, and 83% share a sign.
  - All four edges keep their sign and nominal significance. Only
    payrolls → Fed clears BH.
  - The consensus is coarse: it is exactly zero on 32–83% of events, because
    it sits on the release's 0.1 tick. So the market-relative measure is the
    more powerful of the two, not a different result.

This is also an answer to identification. The structural claim does not
depend on the surprise being defined relative to the market's own price.

### 3.4 Pooling, magnitude and collapsing releases

- **Channel pooling is the strongest structural result.** Group the pairs into
  channels and impose one theory-fixed sign per channel, fitting nothing. The
  data → policy channel then gives:
  - 598 rows over 53 target-event clusters;
  - **63.2% sign agreement against a clustered null of 50.0% ± 3.4%,
    p = 0.0004.**

  The null flips the sign of all rows that share a target event, since one
  FOMC meeting is the target of every trigger before it.
- **Magnitude is partly recoverable.** August's "direction, not magnitude"
  rested on one comparison (r = 0.686 signed against 0.242 absolute). That
  comparison used an estimator that inherited all of the implied mean's
  error.
  - With a distribution-based measure, the absolute surprise cross-validates
    at 0.588.
  - Restricted to events the ladder could express, surprisal is the best
    measure of any kind (0.526 / 0.505), ahead of the signed surprise.
- **Collapsing releases.**
  - Treating the four CPI ladders as one factor does not pay: it buys rows and
    loses correlation.
  - A labour index (payrolls z minus unemployment z) does pay, and it
    rehabilitates the unemployment rate. That gain is confounded with the
    sample period.

### 3.5 Still a hub, not a web

Every surviving edge points at the policy path. There is no interior node, so
multi-hop message passing has nothing to traverse. The mediation test proposed
in August agrees: every partial correlation is unchanged to within ±0.04. §7
shows the same from four more directions.

## 4. Direction prediction: a result, and its withdrawal

A Stage-2 study asked whether the estimated structure predicts the *direction*
of the target's next move out of fold. Its design:
- dormant horizon;
- direction label;
- edges re-estimated inside every walk-forward fold, on training rows only.

- **On 7 September it reported that the structure predicts direction:**
  63.0% against a 59.3% base, p = 0.041.
- **On the corrected panel (§2) that is a null.** The sign rule reaches 58.7%
  (p = 0.23, n = 92). The point estimates barely moved, but the samples
  collapsed, because a usable row now needs a pre-trigger print on the leg
  actually used.
- **The rung that appeared to win instead was an artefact.** It was reading
  the target's pre-trigger *price level*. Kalshi prices are bounded, so from a
  low price the next move is mechanically more likely to be up. Removing that
  one feature takes it from 54.4% to 49.2%.

**Reading:** on the corrected panel, neither the structure nor the surprise
predicts direction at the dormant horizon. The machinery is correct: it is
tested, including planted-signal recovery and a pure-noise null. The result is
withdrawn.

## 5. Economic significance

### 5.1 At the release horizon: significant, not exploitable

- **The edge is a jump, not a drift.** The target reprices once, completely,
  at its first print after resolution. From the last pre-resolution trade the
  signal is worth +0.91c with a 58.7% hit rate. From the first executable
  post-resolution print it is worth +0.47c, with a 38.0% hit rate. **No
  tradable price exists in between, because these books do not print.**
- **Costs then bury it:** net **−3.15c per trade** as a taker (t = −4.31,
  n = 92), and −0.77c under the most favourable maker assumption. Spreads also
  roughly double around releases (median 2.0c within 30 minutes of a release,
  against 1.0c quiet), so even that figure is optimistic.
- **Holding longer does not help.** Gross does not grow with the holding
  period (1 hour to 14 days, and to settlement). Clustered on the target
  event, which is the honest unit, the hold-to-settlement version is
  indistinguishable from zero.
- **Staleness did not manufacture the edge.** If the stale reference price
  created it, accuracy would rise with staleness. Instead it falls
  monotonically (0.630 / 0.622 / 0.450 by the age of the reference). This was
  the CA report's central threat, and it has been checked and cleared.
- **WTI has no information event.** WTI → gasoline CPI is flat (ρ = +0.04,
  n = 80) despite oil passing into gasoline prices by construction. WTI's
  settle is public and continuously observable, so its "surprise" measures
  when the ladder was snapped, not news. **A trigger needs a scheduled
  information release, not merely a resolution time**, and the trigger
  universe now encodes this.

**The answer to the August reframing:** the structure is economically
significant and not economically exploitable at the release horizon. The
friction that sustains the anomaly is the friction that makes it
unexploitable. This agrees with Angelini & De Angelis (2026) on Kalshi.

### 5.2 Five independent efficiency results

Each uses real traded prices, with no distribution reconstructed.

1. **The wings are fair on macro ladders.** Selling the longshot side at
   3–20c nets −0.13c (CI [−1.79, +1.46]), over 4,606 legs on 822 events. The
   only wing mispricing is in WTI's bucket ladders: +4.96c, year-clustered CI
   [+2.26, +5.81]. It survives four attempts to kill it, but it decays from
   +5.81c (2022) to +2.43c (2024). This retires the premise of a proposed
   uncertainty-forecasting line of work for macro series.
2. **The ladders are calibrated.** An earlier analysis reported that outcomes
   land systematically high in the market's implied distribution ("ladders
   biased low"). **That is retracted.** It came from how the distribution was
   reconstructed: open-tail mass placed half a spacing past the last traded
   strike. Interpolating directly between traded strikes, the probability
   integral transform is uniform (CPI 0.529 against the reconstruction's
   0.722); only the Fed ladder rejects. Two things are real:
   - a coverage gap: 19.9% of prints land outside the traded strike range;
   - the Fed ladder's under-dispersion.

   The same method gives the cleanest form of the cross-market response. A
   trigger's resolution shifts the target's median by **+0.043 IQR** (CI
   [+0.012, +0.074]), and leaves its width and skew unchanged.
3. **Coherence violations are real but unprofitable.**
   - Adjacent strikes violate monotonicity on ~2% of pairs, flat across
     synchronicity. The median violation is 2.0c against a ~2.3c cost.
   - The MoM and YoY CPI ladders disagree by a mean 13.1c, and look like
     one unknown priced twice. They are not arbitrage: BLS rounds the two
     independently, so matched legs settle differently 12.8% of the time.
     Net −1.07c.
   - Implied uncertainty decays ~14% into a release, with no variance
     premium.
4. **Maker execution does not rescue the lead-lag signal.** Adverse selection
   costs 1.7–3.1c, against a 0.5–1c spread saving.
5. **The signal does not improve the market's own probability forecast.**
   Neither a walk-forward tilt nor a logistic with the market price as offset
   beats the market's Brier score or log loss, in any price bucket.

## 6. The lead-lag trade, held to settlement

### 6.1 The rebuilt study

The lead-lag question was rebuilt from scratch against a **settlement** target
and the **full target ladder**, rather than one representative leg. The panel
is 10,714 legs, 262 trigger events, 401 target events and 135 ordered pairs.
Same-release pairs are excluded, and the sign restriction has zero fitted
parameters.

- **The signal predicts settlement.** The top minus bottom signal tercile is
  **+3.80pp**, block-permutation **p = 0.0005**. The effect is confined to
  10–75c legs and absent above 75c.
- **Gross +2.36c** a position, against zero for a permuted signal. **Net
  +0.81c**, with a CI through zero.
- **It decays:** +6.46c in 2023, +1.55c in 2024, −0.35c in 2025.
- **Confirmation is the one thing that helps.** Requiring the market to have
  already moved the signal's way lifts walk-forward net to +3.03c. It is
  positive in all three years, and the filter's marginal value is stable while
  the base signal decays.
- **Imposing the relations beats learning them:**

  | relations | parameters | result |
  |---|---|---|
  | imposed theory sign | 0 | +0.86pp |
  | per channel | 15 | +0.47pp |
  | per pair | 135 | −0.07pp |

  0 of 88 pairs survive BH on their own. This is the first of several results
  this month with this shape (§7).

### 6.2 A frozen specification

The full signal → execution chain is written down with every parameter's
provenance (a priori, walk-forward, or chosen in-sample), so that it can be
frozen and tested once:
- threshold ladders only;
- the theory sign;
- walk-forward terciles;
- a confirmation move of at least 2c in the signal's direction;
- an entry-price floor of 20c;
- entry at the second post-news print, taker costs, held to settlement.

**Its headline was corrected twice before being frozen:**
1. **The tie-break.** The original +5.41c depended on how simultaneous trades
   were ordered (§2). Under two deterministic rules it is **+4.71c and
   +4.66c**, and both CIs still exclude zero.
2. **The permutation null.** Shuffling the signal through the *entire*
   specification (terciles refit, filters reapplied) still earns **+1.65c**,
   because the filters are not neutral. The signal's own contribution is about
   **+3.8c**, and the observed value sits 2.7 sd above its null.

Each component earns its place: signal alone +0.53c, rising monotonically to
the full specification. The full version captures about a third of the
perfect-foresight ceiling. A look-ahead audit, alternative exit rules and
sizing all leave the specification unchanged.

### 6.3 Liquidity and propagation

- **Underreaction by liquidity** (Hong, Lim & Stein).
  - Thin legs reprice 30× slower (30 hours to first print, against 1 hour).
  - The edge is larger there in sign, not measurably in size: about +3c
    thin minus liquid, P(≤ 0) ≈ 0.3.
  - A first-pass +7.17c had no confidence interval, and was mostly portfolio
    churn between filter settings.
- **Propagation past the next contract.** Does a surprise stay unpriced in a
  target's *later* contracts?
  - Only labour → Fed has a horizon profile: +0.3pp at the next meeting,
    against **+3.0 / +2.6 / +3.5pp** for meetings two to six months out.
  - It closes within a week: +14.3 → +11.8 → +4.0 → −0.4pp at 0 / 1 / 7 / 30
    days, on 10–75c legs.
  - It survives controls for intervening news (+11.7pp, CI [+1.5, +20.7]).
  - Costed, a far-meeting rule nets +6.24c (p = 0.008). But buying YES on
    every far leg earns +6.30c, which is the Fed cycle (+33.9c in 2022,
    −19.4c in 2025). In-sample, the two cannot be separated. This is reported
    as exploratory.

### 6.4 The out-of-sample test is ready, not run

The 2026 inputs are built. The build reproduces the in-sample legs exactly
(969 of 969 rows on a validation block; the only differences are the
tie-break). Three conventions were fixed in writing before the test:
- the handling of three CPI component series relaunched in April 2026;
- which rows fall in the test window;
- the input path.

**Pre-registered expectation:** a point estimate below ~+4.7c, given the
decay. +2 to +4c with a CI including zero would be consistent with the
in-sample result. A negative result falsifies it. One number will be reported,
whatever it says.

## 7. The spatial-temporal graph question

The CA report's model was an STG. This month tested:
- whether AGCRN's failure was the model's or the implementation's;
- whether any graph learner can recover the structure;
- whether the time axis carries anything a temporal model could use.

### 7.1 AGCRN: run, audited, and still at zero

- **Run as proposed, no model beats predicting zero.** More capacity is worse:
  R² vs zero is ≈ 0 for linear, −0.18 for minimal AGCRN, and −0.61 at the
  290k-parameter default. The learned adjacency ranks **0 of the 4** validated
  edges among its top edges.
- **An implementation audit found real handicaps.** Four were bugs:
  - padded nodes leaked into the graph and the recurrence (69% of the tensor
    was padding; 37–58% of the adjacency's mass sat on padded nodes);
  - the structural prior was transposed;
  - the exported adjacency had trigger and target swapped;
  - one node embedding was pooled across the batch.

  The largest cost was none of these: a randomly initialised output head, kept
  by early stopping at epoch 0, emitted noise. Zero-initialising the head
  alone moves R² from −0.33 to −0.05.
- **With every fix, AGCRN ties the linear ladder, and neither has signal.**
  The best AGCRN scores −0.008 against linear's +0.0002. Even with an oracle
  rescaling, nothing exceeds R² +0.006. The diagnosis is unchanged: the target
  and horizon carry no predictable central tendency between releases.

### 7.2 Graph models against linear on every axis

A new **event-time panel** puts one step at each release instant: 154 instants
× 17 series. It records each market's state just before the release and its
response from the post-release prints. This removes the daily-bar horizon
problem by design. On it:
1. **No graph model beats linear on any metric:** R², accuracy, balanced
   accuracy, F1 or AUC, on either the immediate or the settlement label.
2. **The STG is no better than its parts.** Removing the graph, the history,
   or both never hurts beyond noise.
3. **Signs.** Without the signs supplied, graph models do not learn them
   (balanced sign accuracy 0.44–0.64). Frozen in, AGCRN mostly keeps them.
4. **Gross returns.** The best signal is a linear model of each market's own
   state (+5.2c per trade gross), and that is a price-level effect. No AGCRN
   beats its matched linear model.
5. **Scoping the graph to the one supported channel** (labour → policy) does
   not help. Every AGCRN ranks the immediate label below chance there. The
   zero-parameter sign rule scores AUC 0.76–0.78 and +7.3c per trade over a
   random side.
6. **A Bayesian hierarchical graph** (every edge its own coefficient,
   partially pooled within its channel, with a hard or a soft theory-sign
   prior) stops the sign flips of the free linear graph, but only ties the
   simplest rules. The data supports 3 of 142 edges, and one channel.
7. **Time encoded in calendar units** (lag windows, exponential decay, a
   learned decay gate) carries no signal. AGCRN does best *without* its
   recurrence: each release is fully absorbed before the next one arrives.

### 7.3 Can the graph be learned at all? A recovery test

The test plants the theory graph (25 signed edges among 142 candidates) in
semi-synthetic data. The data keeps the real release calendar, the real inputs
and the real label noise; only the cross-market signal is replaced by a known
one.
- **At the real sample (~5 co-firing releases per edge), no learner recovers
  it:** AUROC ≤ 0.63.
- Simple linear graph learners recover it at **~10× the data** (0.69–0.85).
- AGCRN does not, even at 30× (≤ 0.60).
- The binding constraint is the surprise being diluted among the other input
  features, not the adjacency form.

**Imposing the structure is not a shortcut. At this sample size it is the only
estimator that works.**

### 7.4 More shocks

Three sources of extra shocks were tried, to approach that 10×:
- **Unscheduled price jumps** (2,198) carry no cross-market information.
- **Order-flow bursts** carry little.
- **1,036 scheduled releases with a consensus but no Kalshi market** (6.7×
  the events) do produce significant theory-direction responses.

With them, a learned 15-coefficient channel graph recovers theory's policy
hub: 11 of 15 channels point in the theory direction, 4 are significant and
none go against it. It ties the theory rule out of sample. At edge level (776
coefficients), learning still fails.

### 7.5 Inside the release: an intraday study

The last place a temporal model could help is *within* a release. Every target
was followed on 5-minute to daily bars after 139 Kalshi and 1,030 calendar
releases.
- **Cross-market responses are fast:** half in by 15 minutes, all by about
  two hours. Labour → policy is complete within the hour. The one slow channel
  is labour → growth.
- **Most of the lag is waiting for a trade.** The first post-release print
  carries ½ to ⅔ of the move.
- **There is no propagation through the network.** The source market's own
  move adds nothing beyond the headline surprise. Each market reads the
  release itself, in parallel. This is the identification result August
  asked for: the response is to the public release, not relayed between
  markets.
- **Models.** Once bid-ask bounce is removed, no model beats a linear
  baseline anywhere. This covers a GRU, an intraday AGCRN, LightGBM, a random
  forest, a per-bar MLP, and two Bayesian hierarchical graphs. The one
  learnable piece is the releasing series' own next contract finishing its
  move. One pooled absorption curve predicts it best (24-hour R² +0.13, AUC
  0.68), against 0.59–0.65 for trees, MLPs and the Bayesian graphs. The AGCRN
  stays at predict-zero. The ranking is:

  absorption curve / linear ≥ Bayesian graph ≈ trees ≈ MLP ≥ GRU > AGCRN.

### 7.6 The published literature

Five STG-in-finance papers were reviewed. Their reported gains rest on:
- predicting price levels rather than changes;
- labels partly known at prediction time (in one, a zero-parameter rule
  scores 77–81% on the real series);
- random-day splits;
- no transaction costs.

Where a paper does report an honest out-of-time directional number, it is at
chance, which matches this project. The papers also read learned adjacencies
as economic structure, and §7.3 shows that reading fails under a known truth.

### 7.7 What this adds up to

On every axis, models rank by **how much structure they are given, not how
much they can learn**. The network exists, and it is the textbook one: macro
surprises → policy path. An STG cannot improve on a one-parameter rule here
for four measured reasons:
1. ~5 shocks per edge, where learning needs ~50 (§7.3);
2. markets react to the release in parallel, so there is no message to pass
   (§7.5);
3. the structure is known a priori, so one sign-restricted slope captures it
   (§6.1, §7.2);
4. releases are absorbed within hours, so there is no cross-release lag to
   model (§7.2).

**Proposed framing for the thesis's modelling contribution:** *when is network
structure learnable from event-driven markets, and what is the right
estimator when it is not?* The four points above are the answer, and every
piece of it is already measured. This keeps AGCRN in the thesis as the August
update intended, repositioned rather than removed. Its failure is now
explained by a data requirement rather than asserted.
