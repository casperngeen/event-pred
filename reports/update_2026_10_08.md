# Kalshi sports markets: how information reaches prices, and can it be traded?

*Summary of the sports research, 27 Sep – 8 Oct 2026*

## Summary

1. **Between games, futures absorb results slowly, but not tradably.**
   - Futures are season-long contracts such as a division title.
   - A third of a game result reaches the team's division-title price only after the first post-game trade (t = 5.7), and rivals move the opposite way (t = −4.0).
   - Trading this lag earns about 0.3c per contract before fees, below the cost of trading.
2. **Graphs learned from prices don't recover the known team structure; graphs from metadata do.** Supplying "who plays whom" lets a graph model learn the rival effect's sign: 89% correct, against 0% when it must learn the graph itself.
3. **In-game, taking prices can't beat costs, but market making can.** Market making here means resting limit orders only on the side a play-by-play model favours.
4. **Frozen out-of-sample test** (games from 1 July 2026):
   - **MLB spreads +5.4c per contract (t = 3.9)** and **totals +5.0c (t = 5.1)** after fees.
   - NFL spreads +4.6c (t = 2.2); NFL totals 0.0c.
5. **NFL weakened because the 2026 market got faster.** About 88% of a touchdown's price move now happens before the play-by-play feed records it, against about 60% in early 2025.

## 1. Data and ground rules

- **Kalshi:** about 1.2M MLB, NBA and NFL markets from 2025 on, with every trade and 1-minute bid/ask.
- **Contract types:**
  - **winner:** "Will team X win?"
  - **spread:** "Will X win by more than *k*?"
  - **total:** "Will the teams score more than *k* combined?"
  - **futures:** season-long, such as division, conference and champion.
- **Play-by-play:** MLB every pitch, NFL every play, NBA every possession. Results match Kalshi settlement in all 5,589 settled games.
- **No look-ahead.** Every input carries the time it became known, and a test checks that adding later data never changes earlier rows. This caught two real leaks:
  - MLB plays timed at the pitch start gave a fake forecast R² of 21%;
  - NFL plays were logged out of order in 281 of 282 games (fixed; ≤ 0.1c effect on results).
- **Evaluation:** walk-forward folds (each period predicted by a model trained only on earlier games), then a **holdout** of all games from 1 July 2026. The holdout was sealed, and the rules were frozen in writing before it was read.
- **Game-level t:** each game's P&L is one observation, because trades within a game are correlated.

## 2. Between games: results → futures

**MLB 2025 pilot (2,196 games).** Response of futures to a game's surprise (actual result minus pre-game win probability):

| | Done by first post-game trade | Further drift to +24 h |
|---|---|---|
| Own division contract | 70% | **+1.33c per unit surprise (t = 5.7)** |
| Division rivals' contracts | | **−0.32c (t = −4.0)** |

**Can a graph model learn the known structure?** A win helps your own title odds and hurts your rivals'.

| How the graph was built | Rival edges found (AUROC; 0.5 = chance) |
|---|---|
| Schedule read from market titles | **0.999** |
| Learned from price responses (correlation, ridge, AGCRN) | 0.46–0.57 |

- **Too little signal per edge.** Each pair of teams has about 160 games, but the effect per game is weak (correlation about 0.2 for a team's own contract, about five times weaker for rivals).
- **Standard AGCRN can't learn a negative effect.** Its non-negative adjacency got 0% of rival signs. Supplying the schedule as a separate graph gave 89%.
- **Decision:** take the structure from metadata and let the data estimate sign and size.

**All leagues, linear walk-forward models.** Edge weights that depend on the state lifted out-of-sample R² at 12 h:
- MLB division 0.29 → 0.45;
- NFL division 0.48 → 0.61;
- NBA playoffs 0.16 → 0.30.

The state here is how uncertain the contract is and how much season is left. Forecasts were well calibrated (realised ≈ 0.7–0.9 × predicted), but trades that beat costs averaged −1.3c net (80 trades). **Between-game trading was shelved;** the graph work continues as thesis research (section 7).

## 3. In-game markets: how fast, and what it costs

- **Winner contracts price a play within 30–60 s.** NFL, from the end of the play: 49% of the move by 5 s, 67% by 10 s, 90% by 60 s.
- **Speed tracks trading volume, not the season.** MLB: about 4% of the 60-s move done by 10 s in 2025 (about 3 trades/min) vs 39–67% in 2026 (45–82 trades/min).
- **Taking prices can't pay.**
  - A round trip costs 3.3–4.0c (spread plus two fees); the median 3-minute move is 2–2.5c.
  - Apparent taker profits from rebuilt quotes became −0.4 to −2.7c once entries had to match real trades.
  - **Decision:** forecast and test on traded prices only, and make markets instead of taking.
- **Large "jump" trades** (≥ 30c from the previous same-side price) lose 25c each held to settlement (147k trades): overreaction, not insider trading.
- **Published win probabilities are used as inputs, not trained.** The market beats them on accuracy in every period, and they don't lead price moves.

## 4. In-game model and trading rules (development folds)

**Model.**
- A recurrent network (GRU) reads the plays so far.
- A 2-round graph network passes messages between the game's winner, spread and total contracts.
- It forecasts each contract's traded price 30 s – 2 min ahead. Five seeds are averaged, with a 10 s feed delay.
- **Feed delay** means play-by-play latency only: at decision time *t*, the model may use a play only if the feed recorded its result at least 10 s earlier. The clock starts when the feed records the play, not when it happens on the field.
- **Execution is assumed instant.** Kalshi prices are seen with no lag, and an order is in the book at *t* itself. Real order latency would add costs; it isn't modelled yet.

| Design choice | Result |
|---|---|
| Play memory (GRU) vs none | 60 s R² roughly doubled (NFL spreads 2.7% → 5.2%, totals 1.8% → 8.1%) |
| GRU vs small transformer | GRU better by 0.02–0.05 R² → kept GRU |
| Futures added as graph nodes | NFL totals R² 16.7% → 13.3% (t −7 to −11) → dropped |
| MLB: every pitch as an event (vs each plate appearance) | R² totals 8.8% → 11.5%, spreads 9.8% → 13.1% → adopted |
| Feed delay 0 → 20 s (NFL totals R²) | 8.0% → 1.7%: speed matters |

**Market making.**
- Without a signal: −0.5 to −1.5c per fill (you get filled when the price moves against you).
- With the model's signal: +1 to +3c per fill (NBA t 5–17).

Final rules:
- Post on the favoured side when the 2-minute signal is ≥ 5c, 1c better than the last trade.
- Count a fill only when a later trade prints strictly through our price. This is conservative, since there is no order-book data.
- Exit 2 minutes later at a limit price, else take, else hold to settlement.
- **Size = ((|signal| − 5)/10)² × (width/10)**, each part capped at 1. Width approximates the bid–ask spread.
  - This beat the best hard cut-off (NBA spreads t 6.2 vs 5.0).
  - Per-league minimum signals lost money out of sample (−$0.8), so one minimum is used for all leagues.
- Fees: maker 1.75·p(1−p)c, taker 7·p(1−p)c (assumed; not yet verified with Kalshi).

**When to decide: every 5 s, one order or position per contract at a time.** In MLB, 80% of these trades came while a newer pitch had been thrown that the model hadn't seen yet (the feed delay). Those trades earned about 0c, against about 10c when the model was up to date. Fix: skip decisions 15–60 s after the last known pitch, the window in which a new pitch has usually been thrown.

| MLB, development, net c/contract (t) | Totals | Spreads | Trades with model up to date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every 5 s, one at a time | 2.3 (3.2) | 3.2 (3.1) | 20% |
| **+ skip 15–60 s after last known pitch** | **7.0 (5.1)** | **9.8 (5.1)** | **73%** |

## 5. Out-of-sample test (holdout from 1 July 2026)

**Frozen in advance:** model, seeds, decision rule, sizing, fees, and the six candidates (spreads and totals in each league). **Main result: the fixed exit.**

**Disclosure.** NFL was first run under a once-a-minute rule, a mismatch with the intended rule. It was then rerun under the intended rule. Both are reported.

| Contract | Games | Contracts | Development | **Holdout net c** | At 2× fees | **t** | All 5 seeds positive | Months up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MLB spreads | 1,167 | 422 | 9.8 | **+5.4** | +4.5 | **3.9** | yes | 5/5 |
| MLB totals | 1,167 | 576 | 7.0 | **+5.0** | +4.2 | **5.1** | yes | 5/5 |
| NFL spreads | 64 | 105 | 6.5 | **+4.6** | +3.8 | **2.2** | yes | 1/2 |
| NFL totals | 64 | 94 | 8.0 | **+0.1** | −0.8 | **0.0** | no | 1/2 |
| NBA | — | — | — | *season starts late Oct 2026* | | | | |

- **NFL under the once-a-minute rule:** spreads −2.1c (t −0.5), totals +4.0c (t 1.2).
- **Forecast R² at 60 s, development → holdout:**
  - MLB spreads 13.1 → 11.3%; MLB totals 11.5 → 9.1%;
  - **NFL spreads 12.0 → 4.0%; NFL totals 16.7 → 1.3%.**
- **MLB postseason:** 19 games; too few to judge (t < 1).

**Hold to settlement instead of the fixed exit?** It beat the fixed exit everywhere in development, but not out of sample:

| Holdout, net c (t) | Fixed exit | Hold to settlement | Hold − fixed, paired over games |
|---|---|---|---|
| MLB spreads | 5.4 (3.9) | **9.5 (4.3)** | **+$17.4 (t 2.5)** |
| MLB totals | **5.0 (5.1)** | 4.8 (2.8) | −$1.4 (t −0.2) |
| NFL spreads | **4.6 (2.2)** | 3.3 (0.8) | −$1.4 (t −0.4) |
| NFL totals | 0.1 (0.0) | 2.0 (0.5) | +$1.8 (t 0.6) |

Holding also had 1.6–2× the per-game swings and a 2–4× larger drawdown. The fixed exit stays the main rule; holding MLB spreads is a candidate for paper trading.

## 6. Why NFL weakened

**Execution behaved as in development.** The loss is in the forecasts.

| NFL | 2025 weeks 1–5 | 2025–26 development | **2026 holdout** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Touchdown move done before the play-by-play records it (spreads / totals) | 57% / 61% | 74% / 68% | **88% / 87%** |
| Touchdown move left when the model learns of it | 34% / 32% | 19% / 20% | **9% / 8%** |
| Stale-price rule ("price moves to the bid/ask middle"), R² spreads / totals | 18.5% / 12.1% | 11.1% / 14.3% | **3.8% / 3.2%** |

- **Prices now update almost instantly.** Much of the model's development skill was stale last-trade prices catching up, and that has mostly gone.
- **The model's skill beyond the stale-price rule also fell:** totals 6.9 → 1.5 R² points, spreads 5.1 → 2.2.
- **It isn't early-season football:** the 2025 early season was the *most* predictable period.
- **Speed hurts totals, not spreads.** Delivering plays at 0 s instead of 10 s lifts NFL totals from 0.1c to 4.3c (t 1.9); spreads stay about 4.8c. This used models trained for a 10 s delay, so it likely understates the gain.

## 7. Hypotheses and open research ideas

1. **The in-game edge decays as markets get busier** (NFL 2025 → 2026). *Test:* track paper results over time; retrain on a rolling schedule; check MLB in 2027.
2. **Skill from play information lasts; skill from stale prices doesn't.** *Test:* split MLB's skill the same way as NFL's.
3. **Speed is worth money for totals.** *Test:* models trained at 0, 3 and 5 s delays (ready to run); a feed faster than play-by-play.
4. **Forecasts need recalibrating when markets change.** NFL holdout forecasts were about twice too large (slope 0.57). *Test:* shrink forecasts by a factor fitted on recent games.
5. **Untested execution refinements** (from development only):
   - one shared higher minimum signal (10–12c; NBA t 8.3 → about 10.5);
   - spreading each decision over 12 smaller orders per minute;
   - holding MLB spreads to settlement.
6. **Thesis graph learning:** build the graph whose edge weights are learned from state attributes, extending the linear gains in section 2. Score whether it recovers the known structure.
7. **Explanatory mode (parked):** measure how much of each contract's move follows a play. Not tradable, but it answers "how does information spread" directly.

## 8. Next steps and limits

- **MLB:** retrain on all data with the same settings and paper-trade, mostly in the 2027 season.
- **NFL:** treat as unproven; the rest of 2026 is a second clean test.
- **NBA:** first test from late October 2026.
- **Before live trading:**
  - measure the real play-by-play delay and the order execution latency (the backtest assumes the latter is zero);
  - verify fees;
  - record the order book to check how much size the fills could carry;
  - fix a retraining schedule.
- **Limits:** fills are inferred from trade prints; fees are assumed; sizes are fractions of a contract; several MLB rules were chosen on the development folds, and the holdout is their first independent test.
